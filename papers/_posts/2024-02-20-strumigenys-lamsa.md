# Abstract

We tune a mechanical model of muscle-driven versus latch-mediated spring actuated (LaMSA) systems to biological data from the trap-jaw ant genus <i>Strumigenys</i>, incorporating measured correlations between morphological parameters into the model. Using this biologically grounded model, we identify the performance transition between muscle-driven actuation and LaMSA across a range of relative mandible masses, finding that LaMSA outperforms direct muscle actuation at small relative mandible mass, and that the location and abruptness of this transition depends on which kinematic performance metric is used to evaluate it.
